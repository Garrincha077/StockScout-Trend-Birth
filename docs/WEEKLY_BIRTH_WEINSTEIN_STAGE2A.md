# Weekly Birth: Weinstein Stage 1 → early Stage 2 hypothesis

The supplied 1988 edition of *Stan Weinstein's Secrets for Profiting in Bull and Bear Markets* is a design reference, not a historical validation set. In chapter 3, printed pages 59–64 (PDF pages 69–74), the initial Stage 2 breakout follows a base and a flattening 30-week moving average. Chart 3-5 shows the range ceiling as the entry pivot. Chapter 5, printed pages 150–155 (PDF pages 160–165), adds improving relative strength and unusually high breakout volume. The historical chart examples include Mexico Fund, Anthony Industries, National Semiconductor, and Blocker Energy. These are examples of chart structure, not current recommendations or a winner-only training sample.

`weinstein-stage2a-v3` evaluates only completed weekly bars from the activated Unified manifest and tracked watchlist. It uses no future prices. At least 156 weeks of history are needed to assess a prior resistance zone; a recent 39–130-week nontrending base must be present. The base can be as deep as 50% to avoid dropping a post-crash base solely for its old low. Recent consolidation is measured over the 12 weeks preceding the eight-week launch window (at most 14% close range); the older 26-week measurement remains visible for comparison. MA density, extension and launch advance remain explicit checks.

The new confirmation fields are:

- **30W turn:** current four-week SMA30 change is at least -0.25%, prior four-week change at most +1%, and the slope is improving by 0.15 percentage points or has flattened near zero. This rejects obvious mature Stage 2 continuations.
- **Mansfield RS:** the latest stock/SPY ratio relative to its trailing 52-week mean, and its four-week change. A candidate needs nonnegative RS rising at least 0.25 percentage points, or RS near zero and rising at least one point. A tight Stage 1 watch can still qualify with RS at least -4 and rising 0.5 points; a missing benchmark never becomes positive evidence.
- **Fresh trigger:** first breakout above the prelaunch base ceiling within two completed weeks, plus current weekly volume at least twice the prior four-week average. A weaker or stale breakout is excluded from the shortlist.
- **Runway:** closest earlier local swing high more than 1% above the base pivot, measured from the greater of current price and pivot. At least 12% runway is required. An absent swing is called blue sky only with the required history **and** price within 5% below the pivot (or above it); otherwise the level is unverified. This prevents a stock far below an old high from being labeled blue sky.

These numeric thresholds are explicit starting hypotheses. They have **not** been fitted to the book's successful examples. Before promotion, inspect at least 25 current weekly charts, including excluded names and failures, over five market sessions. Record false positives, false negatives, base length, RS, MA turn, volume and overhead resistance. Compare v2 and v3 shortlists on the same point-in-time sessions and keep Telegram in legacy mode until the release gate passes. The `legacy` view/alert switch and historical snapshots remain the rollback path.

The shadow audit includes a `nearMissReviewQueue` of up to 25 base-bearing exclusions, preferring names with chart history in the snapshot and then ordering by fewest failed checks and score. It records `chartAvailable` on each row. This is a manual chart-review queue, not a candidate list or an automatic relaxation of the gates. A zero-name shortlist is therefore inspectable rather than silently treated as success.

## v4 Bottom / Crash Base calibration (draft, shadow only)

`weinstein-stage2a-v4-bottom-crash` gives Weekly Birth its own chart source order: Bottom Fishing's split-only weekly history, then Next and Ryan when Bottom has no chart. Kell Daily keeps the adjusted Next → Ryan → Bottom order. Crash Base membership is read from Bottom's own triggered setup row before mode fields are merged; a score or a similarly named Next setup cannot substitute for it. For a triggered Crash Base with at least 52 weeks of Bottom-detected base age and a five-year drawdown of at least 35%, the model can use a separately measured 26-week launch shelf when the ordinary 39–130-week range detector fails. Its shelf depth must be at most 45% and its price path must not be a one-way trend. All current MA, RS, extension, volume and overhead checks still use completed weekly bars; Bottom's detector score is evidence, not automatic eligibility.

One data-basis limitation remains visible in `chartSource`: Bottom publishes split-only OHLCV while the reconstructed session SPY benchmark comes from Next/Ryan's adjusted RS column. For Bottom-only names, this makes Mansfield RS an approximation when dividends or other adjustments matter. The shadow review must compare those readings with a chart before promoting the model; an unavailable benchmark never counts as improving RS. No new external price feed is introduced to conceal the mismatch.

The first 6% above a measured pivot is treated as one entry **zone**. A tiny old swing high inside that zone is no longer called a separate wall. The next earlier swing high above the zone remains the modeled overhead resistance. For a prebreakout Crash Base watch, runway is also measured from today's price when it is within 8% below the pivot; the pivot distance and next-zone distance remain separately visible. A breakout must still have at least 12% runway from the pivot/price. This is a transparent swing-high proxy, not volume-at-price supply analysis. The 6% zone and 12% runway are calibration hypotheses, not optimized cutoffs or a promise that overhead is clear.

Bottom Crash Base names can enter the lower-confidence Stage 1 watch with a dense weekly MA cluster, tight recent closes, improving (even mildly negative) Mansfield RS, flat-to-gently-rising 30W average, price close to the pivot and limited extension. A mature surge still fails the same extension, launch-age and fresh-breakout guards. Within the same stage, the shortlist orders qualified Bottom Crash Base names first, then other qualified Bottom names, then Next/Ryan-only names; it never fills an empty list with unqualified names. The shadow audit records chart-source counts, how many Bottom Crash Base names were detected, eligible and selected, and a separate 25-name Bottom Crash near-miss review queue with rejection reasons. This queue never sends alerts. The old v3 2026-09-25 review remains a baseline, not v4 validation.
