# Oliver Kell — Victory in Stock Trading
## Compact project reference

**Source:** Oliver Kell, *Victory in Stock Trading* (2021).

**Use:** default Kell reference for StockScout / Trend Birth work. Prefer this file over the full PDF for routine coding, calibration, candidate review, and discussion. Open the PDF only for annotated chart examples, exact wording, or ambiguous edge cases.

**Scope:** PDF-derived summary only. Numeric thresholds not explicitly stated by Kell must be labeled as StockScout/project proxies.

---

## 1. Trading approach

Kell describes himself as an **intermediate-term trend follower and swing trader**.

Core hierarchy:

- **Price first**
- **Volume as a clue**
- **Moving averages to manage/trail the trend**
- **Multiple timeframes**
- **Chart patterns**
- Strong growth, theme/story, liquidity, relative strength and enough volatility to move
- Risk management first; intuition/feel may supplement rules but must remain inside controlled risk

Primary moving averages:

- 10 EMA — all timeframes
- 20 EMA — all timeframes
- 50 SMA — daily
- 200 SMA — daily

Timeframes: 5m, 15m, hourly, daily, weekly, monthly.

Key principle: **capture the higher-timeframe trend while defining risk with a lower-timeframe entry.**

---

## 2. Cycle of Price Action

### Uptrend sequence

**Reversal Extension → Wedge Pop → EMA Crossback → Base n' Break → Exhaustion Extension**

### Downtrend sequence

**Exhaustion Extension → Wedge Drop → EMA Crossback → Base n' Break → Reversal Extension**

This is a **framework, not a rigid state machine**. Steps may repeat, blend or be skipped. The goal is to understand structural location, avoid chasing, stay aligned with the trend and define better risk/reward.

---

## 3. Reversal Extension

Bullish Reversal Extension is a capitulation/turning-point setup.

Kell looks for:

- defined higher-timeframe support;
- price extended from lower-timeframe 10 EMA ("air" between price and EMA);
- reversal bar;
- heavy-volume capitulation.

Trade logic:

- enter as reversal develops;
- stop below reversal area/bar low;
- first target can be trading-timeframe 20 EMA;
- after partials, move risk toward cost.

Can also be used on a lower timeframe to execute a higher-timeframe pullback/base.

---

## 4. Wedge Pop

Bullish Wedge Pop is the first important **recapture of short-term moving averages** during repair.

Source characteristics:

- price works lower in a tight range;
- often relative strength versus the index;
- 10/20 EMA tighten;
- momentum compresses;
- price recaptures the tight 10/20 EMA.

Entry/management:

- buy the recapture / breakout through the EMA cluster;
- stop below near-term consolidation or pop-day low;
- then use EMAs as trailing guides.

Important: **Wedge Pop is not merely "price above EMA10/20".** Sequence, tightening and prior repair matter.

---

## 5. EMA Crossback

**Source definition: the first retest of the moving averages following a Wedge Pop.**

Sequence:

1. price already recaptured 10/20 EMA;
2. price pulls back into them **for the first time**;
3. EMAs provide a low-risk support area.

Trade logic:

- buy against 10/20 EMA;
- stop below EMAs / crossback low;
- trail stops higher with price/EMAs.

Downtrend mirror: after a bearish break, rally into 10/20 EMA can act as resistance.

### Project caveat

Kell does **not** publish a fixed maximum percentage undercut of the EMA cluster. Any such threshold is a **StockScout proxy**.

---

## 6. Base n' Break

A Base n' Break is a **longer consolidation** than a short 1–3 day EMA pullback.

Source characteristics:

- basing pattern;
- support around 10/20 EMA;
- risk can be defined against EMAs/base;
- buy against averages and/or add on breakout.

Management:

- stop on loss of relevant EMA/base support;
- ride averages higher;
- reduce into extensions away from 10 EMA;
- add on reconfirmation if structure remains healthy.

Higher-timeframe alignment improves quality. Later-stage bases while far above weekly 10 EMA have worse odds.

---

## 7. EMA Pullback Entry

Not necessarily a separate Cycle step.

- shorter than Base n' Break;
- often 1–2 days;
- pullback toward EMAs;
- can be used to add or raise stops.

Warning: **the more extended/mature the trend, the lower the odds another pullback entry works.**

---

## 8. Exhaustion Extension

Represents price stretching away from moving averages.

Interpretation depends on trend maturity:

- **first extension**: often can be held through;
- **second extension**: often a profit-taking area;
- **third extension**: rare and frequently late in the intermediate trend.

Higher-timeframe context matters, especially extension from weekly 10 EMA.

Supporting clues in examples:

- large distance from 10 EMA;
- heavy volume;
- gap and fade;
- bearish engulfing;
- shooting star;
- accelerating / rising-wedge behavior.

Do not reduce this to one fixed EMA-distance threshold.

---

## 9. Wedge Drop

Bearish counterpart to Wedge Pop.

Often follows exhaustion and represents:

- meaningful drop through short-term moving averages;
- failure of intermediate uptrend;
- confirmation that prior exhaustion mattered.

Common logic:

1. stock extends;
2. tries to wedge/hold higher;
3. fails to build a proper new base;
4. loses 10/20 EMA;
5. intermediate trend is broken/requires repair.

Often a sell signal, especially with heavy-volume earnings gap-down or bearish higher-timeframe evidence.

---

## 10. Relative Strength

Central to Kell's name selection.

Relative strength means a stock:

- rises while index falls, or
- falls less than index, or
- makes a higher low while index makes a lower low.

Corrections are especially valuable because they expose future leaders.

Relative weakness is a warning when the index rises but a stock lags or repeatedly fails to sustain highs.

---

## 11. Volume and "Bull Snorts"

Kell calls unusually heavy volume signatures **Bull Snorts**.

Source idea:

- enormous volume relative to normal activity;
- likely meaningful institutional participation;
- a clue to pay attention and then search for a low-risk entry.

The PDF discusses this qualitatively. Exact RVOL cutoffs are not canonical from the book unless separately sourced.

---

## 12. Name selection

Preferred characteristics:

### Growth
- strong sales growth;
- strong earnings growth;
- potential major leadership.

Kell references O'Neil's **25%+ sales growth** benchmark.

### Theme/story
Strong themes can attract sustained demand and help conviction during constructive pullbacks.

### Liquidity/beta
Kell prefers liquid stocks, often around **1M shares/day or more**, with enough beta/volatility to move and with clean/uniform price action.

### Avoid penny stocks
He does not trade stocks under $10 and says his usual names are much higher priced. Institutional sponsorship is important.

### Top Idea combination
Strong price + earnings/sales growth + RS + strong theme + understanding of the company/story.

---

## 13. Gap-ups and catalysts

A gap can mean the **Street was caught off guard**.

Strong constructive context:

- gap through resistance;
- catalyst such as earnings;
- major weekly/monthly breakout;
- heavy volume;
- unfilled gap.

A major catalyst gap may be buyable with the gap-day low as risk.

But cycle location matters:

- early-cycle gap can be strength;
- gap in an already extended stock can be exhaustion.

Kell's principle: **trade the reaction to news, not your opinion of the news.**

---

## 14. Supporting chart patterns

### Bull Flag
Short continuation pattern of lower highs/lower lows; triggers through descending highs.

### Bull Pennant
Lower highs + higher lows + declining volatility; upside breakout trigger.

### Flat / Large Base
Can be initial, continuation or re-initiation buy point. Large monthly bases are especially important.

### Double-Bottom Base
Multi-week base with 2B-reversal/shakeout around lows before regrouping and breakout.

### Descending Channel
Multi-week lower highs/lows; may resolve into Wedge Pop / Base n' Break.

### Ignition Bar
Wide-range and/or heavy-volume continuation bar. Its low becomes an important risk/pivot reference.

### Candlestick confluence
Bearish/bullish engulfing, shooting star, outside reversal, wick rejection, etc. Kell stresses the psychology behind the candle rather than pattern names alone.

---

## 15. Too Tight For Too Long (TTFTL)

A tight base can become a trap when:

- it stays extremely obvious/tight for too long;
- the market/index advances;
- the stock shows relative weakness.

"Tight" is not automatically bullish. Relative performance and context matter.

---

## 16. Stops and selling

Core rule:

**If the reason for buying is no longer valid, the trade is invalid.**

### Breakout-day low
Strong breakout, especially on heavy volume → breakout-day low is an important stop reference.

### Ignition-bar low
After a strong ignition bar, raise stop toward that bar's low.

### Reconfirming Price Strength
Price rises → rests/pulls back → clears the highs that began the pullback. Raise stop below the new pivot low.

### 10/20 EMA trailing stop
Kell often exits after a close below the moving average the stock has been respecting. Which EMA matters is contextual.

### Sell into strength
Take partial profits into extensions rather than waiting until weakness forces the sale.

---

## 17. Market regime / protecting capital

For growth stocks Kell commonly uses NASDAQ / **QQQ** as market context.

When QQQ is **below the 20 EMA**:

- be cautious;
- raise cash;
- become far more selective with new longs.

### Shorts
Kell finds shorting harder. If shorting:

- smaller size;
- tighter stops;
- quicker profit-taking;
- expect violent countertrend rallies.

Corrections should also be used to discover the next long leaders via relative strength.

### Margin
Use only when progress is being made and conditions are highly favorable. Kell explicitly warns inexperienced traders about margin.

---

## 18. Position sizing and portfolio construction

Kell commonly describes **8–12 names**.

Categories:

- **Top Idea** — usually 2–3 best names, early trend, big base, excellent theme/story, strong volume;
- **Conviction Core Idea** — high conviction but below top tier;
- **Volatile Conviction Core** — similar quality but smaller size because volatility is higher;
- **Core Name** — sustained trend holding, second tier;
- **Swing Position** — shorter flags/pennants/EMA pullbacks, usually quicker profit-taking.

Kell notes ATR would be a valid way to normalize volatility even though his own sizing is more discretionary.

### Buying/selling in pieces

Major principle:

**buy in pieces and sell in pieces.**

As the trade proves itself:

- add size;
- raise stops;
- use open profit to finance additional risk;
- increase potential reward without proportionally increasing principal capital at risk.

---

## 19. Psychology / process

Key behavioral ideas:

- accept controlled risk;
- arrogance after success is dangerous;
- flexibility when wrong;
- patience when right;
- recognize your emotional tendencies;
- do not average losers;
- prepare your own ideas before the trading day;
- review your own trades so success/failure can be learned and replicated;
- protect capital because there is always another trade.

## 20. Project interpretation: SCREEN ≠ STAGE ≠ SETUP

This is a **project organization of Kell's framework**, not an additional Kell formula.

### SCREEN — why the name deserves attention
Examples:
- relative strength;
- unusual/heavy volume;
- major gap;
- leadership/highs;
- growth/theme;
- liquid high-beta leadership.

A SCREEN is discovery, **not automatically an entry**.

### STAGE — where price is structurally
Primary cycle states:
- Reversal Extension
- Wedge Pop
- EMA Crossback
- Base n' Break
- Exhaustion Extension
- Wedge Drop
- transition / trend support / repair when no clean event exists.

### SETUP — actionable low-risk location now
Examples:
- Wedge Pop recapture;
- first EMA Crossback;
- Base n' Break;
- major buyable gap;
- EMA pullback / continuation pattern.

A high-quality leader can have an excellent SCREEN and a poor current SETUP.

## 22. Calibration priorities implied by the book

When validating computerized Kell setups, prioritize:

1. **structure before indicator state**;
2. **sequence**, especially first retest for Crossback;
3. **cycle location** — early vs late;
4. **higher-timeframe context**;
5. **relative strength**;
6. **volume**;
7. **nearby structural invalidation**;
8. **avoid chasing extension**.

## 23. Compact setup table

| Concept | Precondition | Trigger / structure | Risk reference | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reversal Extension | decline + higher-TF support | reversal while extended from 10 EMA, preferably heavy volume | reversal low | possible bottom |
| Wedge Pop | repair/tightening | recapture tight 10/20 EMA after working lower | consolidation/pop-day low | possible new trend |
| EMA Crossback | Wedge Pop | **first** pullback into 10/20 EMA | EMA/crossback low | low-risk continuation |
| Base n' Break | established trend | longer base at 10/20 EMA + breakout | EMA/base | continuation/add |
| EMA Pullback | trend | short pullback to EMA | EMA/pivot | tactical add |
| Exhaustion Extension | mature trend | stretched from 10 EMA | contextual | profit-taking/top risk |
| Wedge Drop | exhaustion/failure | loss of 10/20 EMA | broken structure | trend end/repair |

## 24. Compact decision flow

For a long candidate:

1. Is it a leader by growth/theme/liquidity/RS/volume?
2. What is the market regime, especially QQQ vs 20 EMA?
3. Where is price in the Cycle of Price Action?
4. Is there a current low-risk setup?
5. Where is structural invalidation?
6. Is price already extended?
7. Does the weekly timeframe support or contradict the setup?
8. If it works, can stops be raised and size added with open profits?
9. If the reason for buying fails, exit.

## 25. Source principles to preserve in code

- **SCREEN ≠ STAGE ≠ SETUP**
- Relative strength during corrections is a major leadership clue.
- Volume identifies institutional activity but does not alone define an entry.
- EMA Crossback = **first retest after Wedge Pop**.
- Base n' Break is longer than a quick EMA pullback.
- Later-trend pullbacks have lower odds.
- Second/third Exhaustion Extensions matter more than an early first extension.
- Higher-timeframe context can downgrade an otherwise valid daily setup.
- Gap strength vs gap exhaustion depends on cycle location.
- Stops belong at structural invalidation.
- Buy/sell in pieces and raise stops as evidence improves.
- Qualitative Kell concepts must not be silently converted into "canonical" numeric thresholds.
- Cycle of Price Action is a framework, not a rigid state machine.
